
#Predicting greenhouse tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.) cv. Roble 956 F1 yield from soil and agronomic variables using random forest and XGBoost models

The present study combined an L9 (3³) experimental fertilization design with a low-cost sensing system based on a 7-in-1 sensor and Raspberry Pi 5 to acquire a large volume of soil physicochemical data, which were integrated with agronomic indicators for yield prediction. The study evaluated the effect of different nominal N–P₂O₅–K₂O levels on the agronomic performance of hybrid tomato cv. Roble 956 F1 across three phenological stages and developed predictive models using the Random Forest (RF) and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) algorithms. In addition, the influence of the differentiated fertilization regimes on fruit physicochemical quality was assessed. The integration of an orthogonal fertilization design, an accessible data-acquisition system, and machine learning models represent the main methodological contribution of this study, complemented by evidence generated under greenhouse conditions in the Andean region of Quindío, Colombia. Overall, this approach provides insights into the relationships among soil physicochemical conditions, plant growth, and tomato yield, supporting the development of more efficient and data-driven strategies for crop monitoring and agronomic management.
## 1.1.1.	  Data preprocessing
As part of data preprocessing, duplicate records were identified and removed, and missing values were addressed through data cleaning and imputation procedures to maintain dataset quality and consistency. The resulting machine-learning dataset comprised 574 repeated measurement records collected during the 2024 greenhouse tomato growing season. The experimental design consisted of nine fertilization treatments with three experimental replicates, corresponding to 27 experimental plots and 81 plants. Because explicit plot identifiers were not retained in the historical machine-learning dataset, the individual records could not be retrospectively assigned to the 27 plots with sufficient traceability to perform a verifiable plot-level grouped split.
To reduce the risk of information leakage associated with the original random row-wise partition, the records were therefore ordered chronologically according to measurement date and divided into training (70%) and testing (30%) subsets. Earlier observations were used exclusively for model training, whereas later observations were reserved for model evaluation.
## 1.1.2.	Machine learning models 
All analyses were performed in Python 3.11.8 using Pandas 2.3.3, NumPy 2.2.6, Matplotlib 3.8.4, scikit-learn 1.8.0, XGBoost 3.2.0, Seaborn 0.13.2, and SHAP 0.51.0. The Random Forest and XGBoost models were configured using predefined hyperparameters. Random Forest was implemented with 800 trees (n_estimators = 800), a maximum tree depth of 8 (max_depth = 8), and a minimum of two samples per leaf (min_samples_leaf = 2). XGBoost was configured with 800 estimators (n_estimators = 800), a maximum depth of 8 (max_depth = 8), a learning rate of 0.03, a subsampling ratio of 0.7, a column subsampling ratio of 0.7, an L1 regularization parameter (reg_alpha) of 0.5, and an L2 regularization parameter (reg_lambda) of 1. A fixed random state of 42 was used for both models.
The target variable for the machine-learning models was defined as the cumulative yield obtained from the three harvests, calculated as the sum of the yields recorded at the three harvest events for each experimental plot. Thus, the models were developed to predict the final cumulative yield rather than the yield of an individual harvest.
Additionally, feature importance analysis was performed using algorithm-specific methods to identify the variables with the greatest contribution to yield prediction. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis was also applied to quantify the magnitude and direction of each predictor's contribution to the model predictions.
